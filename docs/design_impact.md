# Ragdoll Choreographer with a Clockwork Top Hat

This design review was made from source inspection, then followed by implementation and isolated physics probes. Numeric scores below judge expected design value and feasibility; they are not playtest results or a measurement of fun.

## Five changes, in priority order

1. **Visible anatomy damage.** Every substantial contact names its body part, advances a persistent injury state, and gives an immediate world-space visual response. Bruising overlays and bright joint markers remain visible after the flash. Separate the skateboard from anatomical scoring. Acceptance: a shin hit changes the shin, healthy replay frames clear damage, and a board drop never earns rider damage. Human check: identify the biggest injured region without reading results.
2. **Readable impact timing.** Use a moderate slower crash baseline, a brief stronger emphasis for meaningful hits, and a real-time refractory interval. Camera and UI advance using presentation time. Bail gets an anticipation sound; contact gets the crunch. Acceptance: hit spam cannot hold the game indefinitely at the slowest speed, pause/retry restore the correct clock, and the camera remains responsive. Human check: describe two sequential impacts correctly.
3. **Distinct physical launch poses.** TUCK is compact and spins quickly, STARFISH spreads its arms and rotates more slowly, and TORPEDO has a narrow, forward-reaching silhouette with little initial spin. Keep connected pose-specific joint anchors. Acceptance: pose widths and launch angular momentum differ, all 12 physical pieces and 10 joints remain present, and the body stays connected under actual simulation. Human check: intentionally attempt a different first-contact region on the next run.
4. **A camera that shows cause and consequence.** Frame the whole body and immediate landing surface closely at bail, emphasize a struck limb without losing the torso, and widen when a cascade spreads. Record injury state alongside replay transforms. Acceptance: primary impacts remain visible, camera travel is bounded, and replay restores both earlier and final injury states. Human check: explain what hit what.
5. **A directed second act and a prompt finish.** Make the crashbreaker aim readable and its impulse directional. Keep it from dragging the detached board as if still attached. Provide a way to settle the claim when action dies out, then connect the injury report to treatment funding. Acceptance: the aimed boost changes the next landing; replay cannot bank a second payout; distant debris cannot create a long inert wait. Human check: identify a missed opportunity and voluntarily retry.

## Cross-scores and failure conditions

| Candidate | Score / 1000 | Why it helps | What would make it fail |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Root: anatomy HUD, three poses, 0.55 crash speed, 0.18 impact emphasis, real-time camera/UI | 910 | Makes the body and the player's physical choice legible with limited new content. | Long or frequent 0.18 pulses turn 19 simulation seconds into a tedious wall-time wait. Enforce the 0.55-second real-time refractory window and keep pulse durations brief. A camera that is technically responsive but too distant still hides the injury. |
| Cascade rival: branching authored lanes, radius 3.4 blasts, 0.65–1.1 fuse, aimed crashbreaker preview | 860 | Preserves meaningful target choice and gives each collision time to register. Smaller blast reach reduces automatic all-map destruction. | Smaller radii and longer fuses can kill propagation instead of making it interesting. Validate actual cart/crate contact paths. Avoid previewing an exact landing trajectory that the chaotic physics cannot honor; show impulse direction and nearby opportunities. A lane must offer a clear target plus enough uncertainty to surprise. |
| Story rival: best-only incremental route claims, $600,000 treatment goal, warm messages from Mom | 800 | Turns anonymous points into a concrete reason to try another route and avoids rewarding replay duplication. | A good first run followed by repeated zero-dollar retries can make experimentation feel punished. Explain the existing route claim and the improvement needed before launch, and calibrate the goal against observed attainable route totals. Keep family scenes brief and warm; avoid repeatedly interrupting crashes or portraying outcomes as medical fact. |

The strongest combined slice is one readable crash with two viable destinations, three visibly different poses, local limb damage, an aimed rescue impulse, and a result that explains both the injury and the newly banked treatment money. More simultaneous explosions are not an adequate substitute for that loop.

## Implemented scope

- `scripts/rider.gd`: connected pose geometry; compact/wide/narrow silhouettes; pose-specific angular momentum; persistent per-limb bruise overlays and joint markers; complete replay damage restoration; normalized aftertouch; capped mass-scaled directional launch impulse with 2.7 upward component; detached board excluded from player propulsion; selectable accessories attached to both preview and ragdoll head without injury tint on the hat.
- `scripts/ragdoll_limb.gd`: board excluded from injury events; incoming normal closing speed required; impact strength uses measured impulse or observed resolved contact-point velocity change; resting supports do not repeatedly score; reported contact positions remain world-space.

Pin joints remain in this iteration to preserve the stable 10-joint topology. These poses therefore alter launch shape and momentum, not anatomical joint limits. Injury state changes appearance, not physical limb capability. The three hat models are loaded when available from the root agent's asset work.

## Validation evidence and limitations

Godot 4.7.2 parsed both owned scripts successfully. An isolated headless test dropped a head and skateboard onto a real static floor, simulated another five seconds after settling, and checked all three articulated poses for finite connected motion over 300 physics frames each. It also checked localized damage, healthy-state replay restoration, unchanged board momentum after a rider boost, and body/joint counts. All assertions passed after the contact fix.

Measured preview forearm spans were about 0.74, 1.65, and 0.32 units for TUCK, STARFISH, and TORPEDO. Initial forward angular speeds were 4.2, 1.0, and 0.35 radians/second. A rotated head drop produced one injury event with measured strength about 13.33 and no subsequent resting-contact injuries; the board produced none.

The probe uncovered first-contact impulse reports of zero even when velocity changed from approximately -11.46 to +1.76 vertically. The contact-normal velocity-change fallback preserves this observed major hit. It also exposed that the contact position is already world-space, consistent with [Godot's PhysicsDirectBodyState3D documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_physicsdirectbodystate3d.html#class-physicsdirectbodystate3d-method-get-contact-local-position). Rotated-body floor probes confirmed the reported normal is world-aligned for the active engine, so it is not transformed a second time.

The isolated probe is `/tmp/stair_impact_probe.gd`; a rotated-contact diagnostic is `/tmp/stair_rotated_probe.gd`. They are temporary diagnostic tools, not substitutes for the repository integration suite. Full-project import initially encountered other agents' unfinished main/rules contracts, while both owned scripts passed their individual checks. Native visual inspection and human playtesting remain required to assess silhouette readability, bruise contrast, pacing, and whether the new crash loop is enjoyable.
