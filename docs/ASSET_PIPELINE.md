# Asset pipeline

The original hero, skateboard, and taxi are generated by [build_hero_assets.py](../blender/build_hero_assets.py). The editable Blender source is [hero.blend](../blender/hero.blend), with separate `hero`, `skateboard`, and `taxi` collections at their export origins. Generated runtime files are `assets/models/hero.glb`, `skateboard.glb`, and `taxi.glb`. Regeneration replaces those files and `hero.blend`; preserve manual Blender edits separately or incorporate them into the generator.

## Rebuild and import

The generator uses Blender Python without downloaded models or textures. This revision was generated with Blender 5.2.2 LTS through the Blender MCP background executor. The ordinary local command is:

```sh
blender --background --python blender/build_hero_assets.py
```

In the inherited sandbox, direct Blender startup crashed before executing Python. The successful route was `execute_blender_code_for_cli`, opening the existing project blend and executing this payload with an absolute project path:

```python
p = '/absolute/project/blender/build_hero_assets.py'
exec(compile(open(p).read(), p, 'exec'), dict(__file__=p))
result = {'completed': True}
```

The generator starts a fresh scene, creates meshes/materials/weights, exports the three GLBs, and saves the editable source. Exports retain skinning and morphs: `export_skins=True`, `export_morph=True`, `export_apply=False`, `export_animations=False`, and `export_yup=True`. Ride animation is procedural in Godot, not an exported animation clip. Blender −Y becomes Godot forward +Z; Godot up is +Y.

Import and validate from the project root:

```sh
godot --headless --editor --import --quit --path . --log-file /tmp/stair-assets-import.log
godot --headless --path . --log-file /tmp/stair-skin-tests.log --script tests/test_rider_skin.gd
godot --headless --path . --log-file /tmp/stair-injury-tests.log --script tests/test_injuries.gd
```

## Character contract

`HeroRig` contains eleven named bones: `pelvis`, `torso`, `head`, `arm_l`, `arm_r`, `fore_l`, `fore_r`, `thigh_l`, `thigh_r`, `shin_l`, and `shin_r`. `HeroSkin` is one connected weighted body surface; face, helmet, gloves, shoes, and other details are separate skinned geometry. Rest arms are separated from the torso except at shoulder bridges. Anatomical neighbor restrictions and a smooth shoulder blend prevent arm motion from pulling the waist or stretching welded armpits into sheets.

[scripts/rider_skin.gd](../scripts/rider_skin.gd) exposes `skeleton`, `skin_meshes`, `bone_ids`, and `region_materials`. It maps each bone origin directly to the corresponding displayed or physical body frame, retaining the imported rest basis but discarding rest translation from the correction. This matters because the separated rest arms have different centers from the physical standby pose. Desired global poses are converted into parent-relative bone poses, so `get_bone_global_pose()` exposes the actual result.

The imported mesh and skeleton global transforms coincide; the test verifies this. CPU deformation uses the bone global pose multiplied by the imported skin bind matrix, weighted per vertex, then transformed into world space. Recheck this invariant if changing export hierarchy or mesh transforms.

`Skater.pose_ride(time, steer, speed=0, pushing=false)` preserves the selected TUCK/STARFISH/TORPEDO upper-body silhouette while solving grounded legs. `set_push_phase(phase)` synchronizes the push cycle; the ground stroke occurs near 0.55 and recovery begins after 0.62. `sync_skin()` refreshes the rig after replay transforms. Bail copies the displayed body frames into eleven anatomical rigid bodies plus the separate board, retaining ten joints. Hats attach through `set_hat(index)` at head-local `(0, .34, 0)`.

## Damage and replay

Body materials use `<region>__<surface>` names, such as `arm_l__cuff`. The prefix selects the damage channel. The runtime shader preserves `base_color` and `roughness_value`, adding a localized UV abrasion/bruise mask through `severity` in the range 0–100. Accessories retain their own materials.

`set_limb_damage(region, severity)` updates a region. `apply_damage_states(states)` replaces the complete visual damage snapshot, restoring omitted regions to zero. Replay must restore transforms and damage, then call `sync_skin()`; damage never disables a limb’s physics.

## Taxi and board contract

Taxi direct child `Body` has one blend shape, `Crumple`. Traffic currently sets it to 0.85 when damaged and zero when restored. This is one authored front-compression preset, not impact-direction-dependent or simulated metal deformation. Glass, lamps, bumpers, and wheels remain separate.

Taxi wheel pivots are `Wheel_FL`, `Wheel_FR`, `Wheel_RL`, and `Wheel_RR`, with local X axles. Front centers are `(±.9163, -.37, 1.04)`; rear centers use Z `-1.06`. The skateboard has a curved deck, trucks, and individual wheels. Both models retain simple gameplay collision proxies rather than mesh collisions.

## Evidence and visual limits

The final skin suite passed 210 assertions: connected body topology, normalized weights, 5,973 imported surface vertices with multiple nontrivial influences, zero arm-to-hip drag, foot contacts, eleven damage channels, replay restoration, taxi contracts, and zero measured first-bail vertex discontinuity for all three poses. Counts are assertions and imported surface vertices, not independent test cases or unique welded vertices.

`builds/hero_preview/pose_1.png` is the final corrected Blender CPU studio preview using exported Godot bone poses. Studio and reconstructed gameplay renders are not native Godot screenshots; their lighting and rendering differ. Native GPU appearance, frame rate, and human-rated animation quality require an actual interactive playthrough.
