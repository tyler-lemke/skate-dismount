# STAIR//WRECK — character, traffic, and claim-screen art direction

## What the current evidence shows

The [asset studio preview](screenshots/blender_assets.png) reads as a collection of softened primitives: a cuboid jacket, cylinder wheels, blocky taxi, flat-faced crates, and cart. `blender/build_assets.py` confirms that most forms are beveled boxes, low-segment spheres, and cylinders with solid materials. The clean palette is useful, but the skater has little face, fabric, shoe, or board construction to reward a close camera. At the [Night Market ready camera](screenshots/level_2_ready_validation.png), the rider is small against repetitive building slabs; the hat identifies them more clearly than their body or skating action. In the [Civic crash preview](screenshots/level_1_crash_validation.png), the large foreground cart, 11-row injury card, receipt card, aim card, payout card, fund card, speed card, and resource card compete with the fall. The [results preview](screenshots/level_1_results_validation.png) doubles the payout in the pinned HUD and centered report, and world geometry remains visible through the overlapping translucent surfaces.

These are Blender validation renders made from exported Godot geometry, camera transforms, and UI bounds. Blender substitutes fonts, lighting, and color processing; they are **not native Godot screenshots**. `docs/VALIDATION.md` reports no native WindowServer view or human playtest. The critique above concerns visible composition and source geometry; native readability, pacing, rendering, and fun remain gates to check in the actual app.

## One visual language: a handcrafted insurance-claim diorama

Keep the mint/ink/orange/paper identity, but make the city a tactile, illustrated stunt set rather than a low-poly placeholder. Hero surfaces should have deliberately modeled construction: rolled seams, inset glazing, layered deck, laces, cloth panels, stamped decals, and a few restrained wear marks. Let the ordinary city be quiet blue-gray/cream. Reserve saturated mint for player agency, amber for claimable opportunities, and orange-red for active fuses or injury. At a glance, the viewer should know which thing they control, what can cause the next chain, and what has already paid. No blood or grotesque injury effects; the story's urgency comes from the treatment fund and a warm relationship with Mom.

Use a common material recipe instead of adding detail indiscriminately: broad rough shapes, one accent trim, one dark structural inset, and a small high-contrast decal. Give each route a distinct environmental rhythm—Civic's public masonry and domino rails, Market's fabric canopies and warm lanterns, Port's striped industrial cargo—but keep identical control and payout colors across routes. In native Godot, assess exposure/tonemapping and contact shadows before judging the Blender beauty render. Do not let a beauty close-up approve an asset that fails at the gameplay camera.

### Hero assets and implementation contracts

| Asset | Visible target | Runtime contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Skater | Intentional silhouette at ready, riding, and sideways ragdoll angles: a shaped helmet and readable face plane, asymmetric jacket with sleeve cuffs and safety pads, distinct shorts/pants, visible knee and elbow breaks, two recognizable shoes. Design one memorable rear view because the default camera mostly sees the rider from behind. Hats remain playful, small enough not to hide the helmet. | The current seven reusable anatomical GLBs (`head`, `torso`, `pelvis`, `upper_arm`, `forearm`, `thigh`, `shin`) instantiate as 11 named physical regions, with left/right reuse for the limbs. Preserve those region IDs, collider positions, and the `Skater.offsets`/`place_segment` handoff while prototyping; keep the board independent. A premium skinned hero would replace this visual binding deliberately, while retaining the eleven-body injury and claim mapping. |
| Board | Curved nose and tail, grip edge, layered wood, trucks, four distinct wheels, and a restrained graphic readable in a moving rear three-quarter view. | Preserve its approximate 0.76 × 1.65 m deck envelope, independent board body at bail, and sole/deck clearance. Verify push/coast animation contact when that work lands. |
| Moving vehicles | Choose a compact city taxi as the primary bottom-of-stairs chain target. Replace the block-car outline with a hood/cabin/trunk silhouette, angled windshield, wheel wells, lamp clusters, roof sign, and readable side stripe. A second colorway or delivery van can add variety later. | Keep a collision proxy close to the present 1.85 × 1.45 × 3.3 m taxi and a stable `CrashProp` ID/value path. Each moving car stays the **same finite-mass `RigidBody3D`** throughout, driven by bounded forces and torques; a meaningful impact cuts motor and steering control whether or not it is player-caused. Normal driving alone earns no claim. A player-caused car can strike and damage a previously inactive victim, which then becomes causally eligible for payout. Avoid visual-only traffic and any kinematic-to-rigid swap. |
| Domino/carts/drums | Larger distinct profiles and materials: domino edge thickness/pips, market-cart handles and suspended produce, drum rolled rims and a legible arming fuse. | Reuse the current collision/value system; keep the fuse/readiness state distinguishable from a paid wreck. Do not add fragment meshes that replace or desynchronize the intact physical body without replay support. |

The current `rider.gd` transfers each visual region's global transform to a `RagdollLimb`, creates ten joints among the 11 body parts, and copies per-part damage into visible overlays/markers. There are two viable visual paths, with different costs:

1. **Articulated first prototype:** remodel the seven reusable anatomical GLBs around the existing local origins and pivots. Use purposeful pad, cuff, collar, hem, glove, and shoe overlaps to hide joints in every pose. It is the fastest way to prove silhouette, camera scale, collision envelopes, and the first bail frame. Its visible segmentation may still prevent the premium continuous-human look the user wants.
2. **Recommended final hero:** author a properly skinned character rig in Blender, with a continuous jacket/body silhouette and shoes and hand visual bones attached to their corresponding regional bodies. Map the render skeleton onto the existing 11 physical bodies during ragdoll, retaining stable region names, ten physical joints, collision proxies, and localized injury masks/decals. This is **not a drop-in GLB swap**: it needs an explicit ride-to-bail pose handoff, per-frame physics-to-bone mapping, controlled skin deformation near joints, hat/head attachment, and replay restoration of both transforms and damage state. The guide rig and region map must be tested before final sculpt/detail.

Prototype motion and pivots with the present parts first, then use that proven contract to choose the final skinning topology. A joined beauty model simply hidden at bail would pop into a different pose and lose precise injury feedback. Validate all three selectable poses, the first bail frame, later joint rotations, all 11 highlighted injury regions, and recorded replay. Injury visuals should use localized pad/cloth scuffs or masks plus the accessible text stage; never tint the entire skater red to stand in for a specific limb.

### Blender MCP production loop

Keep `blender/stair_wreck_assets.blend` as the editable source and `blender/build_assets.py`/`build_hats.py` as reproducible exports, but replace individual primitive construction in slices. Through Blender MCP's background executor: (1) block the skater, board, and taxi to the Godot metric dimensions and pivots; (2) apply consistent bevel/weighted normals and UV/trim/decal materials; (3) export the seven reusable segment GLBs for the prototype or an explicitly mapped skinned rig for the final hero; (4) run the current Godot import and test suite; (5) generate both a neutral turntable and exact gameplay-camera previews; (6) inspect a **native Godot** run before accepting color, readability, or polish. Preserve the current source kit until each replacement passes. An art-only export should not silently alter masses, payout values, joint anchors, or route timing.

At 1440 × 900 and at a reduced 1280 × 800 view, a person should identify the board/skater, the next target, and the taxi's direction within two seconds, without relying on labels. At a typical crash camera distance, each shoe and arm should remain distinguishable from torso, and a severe injury should visibly belong to its named region. Compare still frames and a short 60 fps capture; a good single Blender render is insufficient.

## Screen hierarchy: the world is the main event

Make **current insurance payout** the only persistent large number. Show the treatment fund as a thin, labeled progress track rather than a second large number while playing. Show **one** crashbreaker token, not three pips. Display limb detail only when it can explain an actual hit. Show upcoming traffic through physical lane markings, headlights/turn signals, and a small in-world bottom-plaza warning; do not add a minimap or another fixed dashboard. A single persistent HUD band should use the outer safe margins; leave the center and rider landing area open. Opaque paper cards belong in menus/results; live play gets compact, high-contrast chips with a consistent baseline and restrained alpha.

| State | Primary visible information | Secondary/contextual information | Remove or defer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Menu | One strong title/hero composition, three route choices, fund progress toward Mom's treatment. | Selected route's payoff and defining targets, warm Mom line. | Duplicate boxed route text and distant route panorama details that cannot be read. |
| Ready | Rider and three pose silhouettes/cards in a compact left rail; charge action near the rider/board. | Selected hat, lane direction, one crashbreaker token, traffic destination visible below the steps. | Speedometer at zero, claim receipts, the full 11-row injury file, and repeated key lists. |
| Run | Payout at upper left; lean charge/speed feedback only when relevant; selected lane/near target in-world. | A one-line prompt for bail. | Large dark blocks at all four corners; treatment fund as a competing sum. |
| Crash | Payout increases with short receipt callouts; **one** crashbreaker token and aim chevron when available; compact body silhouette highlights latest or worst region. | Expand an 11-part injury list only by a hold/toggle or after filing. Real-time traffic direction/warning sits in-world. | Simultaneous three-line receipt box, eleven-row list, speedometer, timer, and multiple instruction cards. Keep a small settle timer/control in an edge chip. |
| Results | Final payout once, property/injury split, fund progress, one clear next-action button. A short warm Mom line anchors why it matters. | Expandable 11-region claim record and replay/retry options. | Background HUD behind the report, duplicate payout, redundant speed/resource/aim labels. |
| Replay | Small `REPLAY` mark, recorded payout, unobtrusive scrubbable or timed progress, visible recorded limb damage. | Exit/retry controls. | Live boost/aim/claim prompts or new-payout language. |
| Pause/help | Opaque enough to isolate controls, scoped action list. | A simple one-crashbreaker explanation and traffic target hint. | Inapplicable phase-specific instructions. |

The existing `game_hud.gd` uses fixed 1440 × 900 positions. Its Ready view is instructive but consumes much of the left and bottom; its Crash view covers the tumbling foreground; Results overlays its center report on still-visible live HUD. First pass should reorganize visibility and typography in the current resolution before attempting a new responsive layout system. Use a single type scale (large payout, medium state title, small metadata), aligned edges, consistent card padding, and an accent-color budget. Ensure a live receipt never covers the rider, traffic cue, or aim marker. Keep text diagnosis alongside color for the 11 injuries. New one-token messaging must be truthful in Ready, Crash, Results, Replay, help, and pause.

## Dependency order and review gates

1. **Lock the rules and camera framing first.** Commit to one crashbreaker per run and define moving bottom-plaza traffic behavior (spawn cadence, lanes, travel bounds, activation/settling, replay state). Confirm ordinary driving and uncaused traffic collisions never earn a player payout; a player-caused car may damage a fresh victim through qualifying physical contact. Sketch the route's target path at the existing Ready, Run, and Crash cameras. Without this, art and HUD callouts may point at the wrong thing.
2. **Build an asset contract scene.** Put the seven reusable rider meshes in all 11 body slots, board, taxi, cart, and lights at gameplay scale with current collider outlines and pivot guides. Prove ride pose, selected bail pose, the exact first ragdoll frame, injuries, and replay with simple parts. Then choose the final skinned rig/handoff and replace the skater/board hero asset. Replace one moving taxi and one chain prop afterward. Review at native gameplay camera, not only turntable distance.
3. **Tune world readability.** Create three distinct bottom traffic lanes with visible motion direction, safe approach/read time, and one clear first chain. Use set dressing to frame, not obstruct, moving vehicles. Verify targets remain visible in all three camera styles and during slow motion.
4. **Reflow HUD around actual sightlines.** Prototype Ready and Crash at 1440 × 900 and 1280 × 800 with maximum real receipt text and all 11 damaged regions, then finish Menu, Results, Replay, Pause/help. Use actual Godot fonts and mouse focus tests. Ensure results hide the live HUD, and one-token state is consistent across prompts.
5. **Playtest before extra decoration.** Ask fresh players to identify the skater/board, bottom traffic direction, how to spend the lone crashbreaker, current claim value, and most damaged limb without coaching. Watch whether they can intentionally hit a moving target and whether a second impact feels attributable to their action. If they cannot, change camera, path speed, target contrast, or HUD density before adding new props.

Acceptance is a short native-video plus source/test evidence package, not a beauty-sheet signoff: all three routes show their traffic cue before the bottom plaza; each moving vehicle remains physically hittable and only pays through causal damage; one crashbreaker is available and consumed once; payout/banking/replay results stay correct; all 11 region overlays and named severities align in the first bail and replay; no rider/model pop or joint gaps at the handoff; UI labels fit both target resolutions without obscuring the rider or upcoming target; a fresh player can explain the next useful action after a single run. `docs/VALIDATION.md`'s current automated proofs remain a baseline, while native appearance, audio, performance, and playability need new observation.
